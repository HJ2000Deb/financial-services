# Q&A pack

Diligence is won by consistency and speed. Every answer is read against every other
answer, by advisers who are paid to find the seam.

## Part one — Anticipate

Before the first question arrives, build the pack from the buyer-flag list in
`debretts-dataroom-index` and the questions the IM will provoke
(`debretts-im-section`). For each anticipated question:

| Question | Short answer | Supporting document (index ref) | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Aim for the sixty questions that account for most of the traffic: EBITDA adjustments,
customer concentration and contract terms, revenue recognition, working capital
normalisation, capex versus maintenance, employee terms and key-person risk, IP
ownership, litigation, related-party transactions, pension, tax enquiries, data
protection.

## Part two — Answer live

For each incoming question:

1. **Classify** — factual, judgement, or refusal. Factual answers come from the room.
   Judgement answers go to the partner. Refusals need a reason and a form of words.
2. **Draft from the document, and cite it.** Every answer names the index reference.
   Answers without a document behind them are the ones that come back.
3. **Check consistency** against every answer already given, the IM and the model. If the
   new answer contradicts an earlier one, stop: someone must decide which is right and
   whether to correct the earlier answer. Correcting it is almost always cheaper.
4. **Route by sensitivity.** Customer names, pricing and employee data go by the agreed
   channel — clean team or Phase 2 — not into an open room.
5. **Log it** and set the deadline. The log is the process record.

## The log

| Ref | Buyer | Date received | Question | Category | Answer | Index ref | Answered by | Date sent | Status | Consistency checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

One log for the mandate, all buyers in it, sorted by buyer for issue. Reconcile daily
while a deadline is live. Before any buyer deadline, produce three counts: outstanding,
overdue, and answered-but-inconsistent.

## What not to do

- Do not answer a question the vendor has not seen, where the answer involves judgement,
  intent, or anything about employees.
- Do not volunteer material beyond the question. Answer what was asked.
- Do not give one buyer materially more than another without recording the decision — an
  auction that treats parties unevenly is a legal problem, not a tactical one.
- Do not estimate. "Not available" is an answer; an invented figure is not.
- Do not send anything. Answers are drafted, reviewed and issued by a human.

## Output

1. The anticipated pack, sixty questions with draft answers and index references.
2. The live log, reconciled, with the three counts.
3. A daily exceptions note: overdue items, inconsistencies found, and questions needing a
   partner or vendor decision.
4. Where the same question is coming from several buyers, a single approved form of words
   for all of them.

## Guardrails

- Every answer traces to a document in the room. `[UNVERIFIED]` never goes to a buyer.
- Legal and regulatory positions go to the firm's legal advisers, not into a Q&A answer.
- No answer contains an internal assessment, a gap-analysis item, or a view on price.
- The vendor approves anything touching employees, customers by name, or intent.
