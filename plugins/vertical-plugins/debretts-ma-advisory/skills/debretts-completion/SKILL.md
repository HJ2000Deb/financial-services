# Close and completion

Most dangerous stage of the deal: the documents are long, the timetable is tight, and a
wrong figure now has consequences. Assembly is Claude's job. Every judgement, and every
legal position, is a human's.

## Negotiation preparation

For each open point, one line each:

| Point | Our position | Their position | What it is worth | What we will trade it for | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then: the three points we will not concede, the three we will trade, the order to take
them in, and what the other side needs from the timetable — the pressure usually runs both
ways, and knowing their board date is worth more than another argument.

## Plain-English document summaries

For the SPA, disclosure letter, W&I policy and any ancillary agreement, produce a summary
for the vendor that a non-lawyer can act on:

- **What this document does**, in two sentences.
- **What the vendor is promising**, listed plainly, with the clause reference.
- **What happens if a promise turns out to be wrong** — cap, basket, time limit, process.
- **Money after completion** — earn-out, deferred, escrow, completion accounts: amounts,
  dates, mechanism, and who controls the measurement.
- **What the vendor must do or not do afterwards** — restrictive covenants, in duration,
  geography and activity.
- **The five clauses to read personally**, with the clause reference and one line on why.

Every line carries its clause reference. **This is a summary for comprehension, not legal
advice** — state that at the top of every summary, and route every legal question to the
firm's legal advisers.

## Completion checklist

| # | Item | Owner | Depends on | Due | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover: signature pages and authority, board and shareholder approvals, funds flow with
account details verified separately by telephone, payoff and release letters, consents and
waivers, employment and service agreements, resignations and appointments, escrow
agreement, disclosure bundle scheduled and dated, data room archived, filings.

Mark the items that block completion and the items that can follow, and never let a
funds-flow detail travel by email alone.

## Post-completion debrief

Within ten working days, internal, honest:

- Timetable: planned against actual, with the reason for each slip.
- Value: expectation at pitch, indicative offers, final. What moved it.
- Diligence: what surprised us, and whether the gap analysis had it.
- Buyer universe: who engaged, who did not, and what that teaches the next mandate.
- Process: what we would do differently, in specifics.
- **Reusable**: what should become a firm skill, and what belongs in the sector Project.

The debrief is the only reason the next mandate is easier. Write it while it stings.

## Output

1. Negotiation grid, with the trade order.
2. Plain-English summaries, one per document, clause-referenced.
3. Completion checklist with owners, dependencies and blockers marked.
4. Debrief note, internal, plus the list of what to capture as method.

## Guardrails

- No legal advice, no tax advice, no interpretation of a clause presented as a legal
  position. Comprehension summaries only, routed to advisers for the position.
- Never restate a figure in a funds flow without checking it against the source document;
  verify bank details by a separate channel.
- Anything Claude summarises from a draft document is marked with the draft's version and
  date. A summary of a superseded draft is worse than no summary.
- The debrief is internal. It never leaves the firm.
