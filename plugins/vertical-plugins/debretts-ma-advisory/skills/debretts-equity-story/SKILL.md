# Equity story

The equity story is the answer to one question: why is this business worth more to a buyer
than the sum of its last three years of EBITDA? Everything in the IM either supports a
pillar of the story or is background.

## Step 1 — Assemble the evidence first

Do not write pillars and then look for support. Read the data pack and list what is
demonstrably true: revenue quality, margin trajectory and its drivers, customer behaviour,
contract structure, market position with evidence, capability that would be slow to
replicate, management depth, and where the growth actually came from.

Anything you cannot evidence from the pack is a question for management, not a pillar.

## Step 2 — Build four or five pillars

Four is better than seven. For each pillar:

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | One sentence, specific, no superlative |
| **Evidence** | Two or three data points from the pack, each with basis and source |
| **So what for a buyer** | The economic consequence for the acquirer, named |
| **The counter** | The strongest argument against the claim |
| **Our answer** | The answer to the counter, or an honest concession |

Pillars that recur in Debrett's mandates and usually survive diligence:

- **Revenue quality** — recurring or repeat revenue, contract length, renewal behaviour.
- **Position in a defensible niche** — share of a narrow market, switching costs,
  accreditation or approval barriers.
- **Margin with a reason** — a specific operational cause, not "operational leverage".
- **A growth path the buyer can execute** — named, costed, and not dependent on the
  vendor staying.
- **Management continuity or a clean handover** — one of the two, stated plainly.

## Step 3 — Test the story

Put each pillar to the strongest counter-argument you can build, as a sceptical buyer
would. A pillar that cannot survive a hostile reading now will not survive diligence, and
it is far cheaper to drop it in preparation than to lose it in week nine.

Then check the story against the numbers a buyer will build: does the adjusted EBITDA
bridge support the margin claim? Does the customer analysis support the revenue-quality
claim? If the story and the model disagree, the model wins.

## Step 4 — Write the investment highlights

Five to seven bullets for the teaser and the IM front page. Each: a claim, a number, a
basis. No adjectives doing work a number should do.

> Not: "Exceptional customer loyalty in a highly attractive market."
>
> This: "94% of FY25 revenue from customers of three years or more; top-ten customer
> retention 100% since FY22 (management data, unaudited)."

## Step 5 — Output

1. The pillar table, complete, with counters and answers.
2. Investment highlights, five to seven lines, teaser-ready.
3. The equity story in one paragraph for the pitch and the process letter.
4. **Where the story is thin** — an internal-only note on the pillars that depend on
   unaudited data or a single customer, and what would strengthen them before marketing.

## Guardrails

- No claim without evidence in the data pack. `[UNVERIFIED]` on anything from management
  that is not yet supported.
- Unaudited management data is labelled unaudited every time it appears.
- Forward statements are the vendor's projections, attributed as such. Debrett's does not
  forecast on a client's behalf in a marketing document.
- The internal note in step 5 never leaves the firm. Check before any external send.
