# Outreach

A generic approach is worse than no approach: it burns the name and teaches the market
that Debrett's writes circulars. One letter per company, built on one specific reason.

**This skill drafts. It never sends.** Drafts land in the author's compose pane or in
`out/`, and a human reads and sends every one.

## Step 1 — Establish the reason to be writing

No draft starts without one of these, stated in a sentence:

| Reason | Example opening line |
|---|---|
| A named event | "You completed the acquisition of Harbrook in April; we advise the two closest remaining independents in that market." |
| A relationship | "James Whitlock suggested we speak." |
| A specific thesis | "We think the compliance-testing market consolidates around three buyers in the next two years, and you are not yet one of them." |
| A live mandate | "We are advising the shareholders of a specialist engineering business, £8m EBITDA, on a sale, and you are one of six parties we intend to approach." |

If none of the four applies, do not write. Find the reason first.

## Step 2 — Draft the letter

Structure, 180 to 220 words for a cold approach:

1. **Why you, why now** — one sentence, the reason from step 1. Names, not categories.
2. **What we know about your market** — one or two sentences with a specific observation
   that could only come from someone who follows it.
3. **What we are** — one sentence. Debrett's, mid-market M&A advisory, the sector, what
   we do. No credentials or transaction history that has not been verified.
4. **The ask** — a twenty-minute call, with two named times. Not "let me know if of
   interest".
5. **Sign-off** — the sender's own name and title.

Rules: no attachment on a first approach; no valuation indication; no "I hope this finds
you well"; no more than one question; British English; the sender's own voice, not the
firm's brochure. If the recipient is a founder, write as one person to another.

## Step 3 — Build the sequence

| Touch | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | The letter above |
| 2 | Day 8 | Two sentences on a new, specific development. Never "just following up" |
| 3 | Day 22 | A useful piece of market content, no ask |
| 4 | Day 60 | Close the loop: "I will stop writing unless it becomes relevant" |

Four touches, then stop and put the name back in the origination sweep. A fifth touch
costs more than it earns.

## Step 4 — Maintain the approach log

Every approach, in one table per mandate or sweep, in the CRM and in the deal folder:

| Company | Contact and title | Route | Touch | Date sent | Sender | Response | Next action | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The log is the deliverable that outlasts the letters. Reconcile it against Outlook before
every pipeline review so that "no response" means no response and not "we forgot".

## Buyer-process outreach differs

On a live sell-side process the approach is a controlled document:

- One approved form of words per buyer category, with a buyer-specific first paragraph
  from `debretts-buyer-universe`.
- No name, no financials and no sector detail beyond the agreed teaser language until an
  NDA is signed.
- The NDA log and the approach log must agree at all times.
- The partner approves the wording once; nobody varies it afterwards without saying so.

## Guardrails

- **No sending.** Draft to the compose pane or to `out/`. Every send is a human act.
- No invented transaction history, credentials, memberships or client names.
- No valuation, multiple or price expectation in any outreach document.
- Check the exclusion and conflicts list before drafting a single letter.
- Personal data stays inside what is publicly filed or already in our CRM.
