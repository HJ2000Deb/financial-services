# Meeting debrief

Notes nobody acts on are a cost. The deliverable is the action list; the record is
secondary.

## Input

Notes, a transcript, a recording summary, or a dictated recollection. Where the input is a
recollection, mark the record as such — memory is not a transcript, and the distinction
matters if the meeting is ever contested.

## Output, in this order

**1. Actions** — first, because it is what gets used.

| # | Action | Owner | Due | Blocks | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules: one owner per action, always a named person, never "the team". A date, never "next
week". Where an action blocks something on the timetable, say what.

**2. Decisions** — what was actually decided, and by whom. Distinguish decided from
discussed; most disputes after a meeting are about that line.

**3. What we learned** — the two or three things that change our view: a number we did not
have, a constraint, an intention, a name.

**4. Open questions** — what we still do not know, and who can answer it.

**5. Record** — the account of the meeting. Attendees, date, what was covered, in the
order it was covered. Neutral register. Quote only where the words matter.

**6. Follow-up drafts** — the emails or letters the actions require, drafted for the owner
to send. Never sent.

## Where things belong afterwards

- Actions → the mandate Project and the deal folder, and into the next process update.
- New facts about the target or a buyer → the dossier or the buyer universe record.
- Anything about intent, price or a term → flagged to the partner, not filed silently.
- New questions → the Q&A pack or the gap analysis.

## Guardrails

- Do not attribute a statement to a named person unless the input evidences it.
- Do not record an inference as something said. "Our read is" belongs in section 3.
- No recording or transcription without the consent of everyone in the meeting.
- Client and buyer meeting records stay in the mandate Project. A debrief that mentions
  another mandate is split before filing.
